            Guide

            What things mean, and how Code Scout helps you find your way. Nothing here needs setting up.

            Every video has a code

            Japanese studios give each release a short code, like NSFS-484: letters for the label (the line of releases it belongs to) and a number counting up within it. Sites write it in different ways (nsfs484, NSFS00484); it’s the same video. The code is the one name every site agrees on, so it’s the surest way to find a video anywhere, and what Code Scout looks for.

            You don’t need to know any codes to start. Search by a name or a word in the popup, browse a site, and the codes come to you: each video you look at shows its code, and Code Scout picks it up.

            What Code Scout adds to pages

            • ▶︎Open the trailer. Next to a code on any page. Click it for the video’s trailer and details; right-click it for more.
            • ★A favourite. You starred this video. It’s in the popup under Favourites.
            • ✓Seen. You’ve opened this one before, so the ones you haven’t stand out. Listed under Recent.
            • ♥A favourite performer. After her name wherever it appears.
            • #A favourite genre. On links to its page.

            What a video is, and what it’s connected to

            In the popup, the arrow at the end of a video’s row opens its card: the title, the cover, when it came out, who’s in it, the studio that made it, the series it’s part of and its genres. Each of those is a way on: click a performer, studio or genre to see more of it, or press ♥ or # to keep it in your favourites. Code Scout reads this from javtrailers.com only when you open a card or star a video, and keeps it on this computer.

            Where things open

            Open trailer goes to javtrailers.com, which has a short preview and the details for nearly every release. To watch the whole video, use Watch on in a video’s card: it looks the code up on the sites below. Add or remove sites under Open on sites.

              Words you’ll run into

              Code
              A release’s ID, like NSFS-484. Also called a DVD ID or product code.
              Label
              The letters of a code. One studio can run several labels, each with its own style.
              Studio
              The company that made the video. Also called a maker.
              Series
              Videos that share a premise or a title, released over time.
              Performer
              Someone who appears in a video. Sites also say cast, actress, idol or model.
              Genre
              What a video is about or how it’s shot. Sites also say category or tag, and each site names them its own way.
              Trailer
              The studio’s short preview of a release.
              Censored / uncensored
              Videos released in Japan are pixelated by law (“censored”); “uncensored” ones were made for release elsewhere, or are leaks.
              Subtitled
              Most releases have no English subtitles. Some sites have subtitled sections, and the video toolbar’s CC can add them.
              Hi-Def, 4K, VR
              How it was filmed. VR releases need a headset to watch properly.
              Compilation / best-of
              Scenes from earlier releases put together, often several hours long.
              NTR
              Short for netorare: stories about a partner being unfaithful. Sites file it near “cheating” and “cuckold”.
              Amateur
              Releases presented as featuring non-professionals. Their codes often start with digits, like 300MIUM-123.

              Quick ways to do things

              • Type in the popup’s box to find anything you’ve saved or looked at; press Enter for the first result.
              • ↓ and ↑ move through a list, → opens a video’s card.
              • Not ready to call it a favourite? Watch later in a video’s card puts it aside under Favourites → Later.
              • The funnel above Favourites and Recent narrows a list to one label, performer or studio.
              • Select a code on any page and use the shortcuts, or right-click it.
              • Prefer denser lists? Switch to Compact under Appearance.

              Shortcuts

              On any page

              Select a code, or put the caret on one in a text box, then:

              • Alt+Shift+J Open trailer
              • Alt+Shift+K Search javtrailers.com
              • Alt+Shift+L Open on all your “Open on” sites
              • Alt+Shift+H Hide what you’re watching, on any page: videos pause, the tabs are muted and go out of sight. Press again to bring them back
              • Right-click On a selected code: Open trailer for “…”, Search trailers for “…”, Open “…” on or Add “…” to favourites. On a link, a performer’s or genre’s link, or the page: open its trailer or add it to favourites. On a ▶ button: more places to open the code
              • Enter Space Press a ▶ button, a chip button or a video toolbar button that has the focus
              • Esc Close the chip or an open menu

              The four Alt+Shift shortcuts can be changed or cleared; the keys below are fixed.

              Video player

              For the video you’re watching, whatever the site’s player, once it has played with sound or while the pointer is on it. Not while you type, and not with Ctrl, ⌘ or Alt held. Switch them off with Player keys.

              • Space K Play or pause
              • ← → Back or forward 5 seconds
              • J L Back or forward 10 seconds
              • , . One frame back or forward (pauses the video)
              • < > Slower or faster, a step at a time, up to 16×
              • M Mute or unmute
              • P Picture-in-picture

              In the popup

              • Enter Open what’s typed in the box, or the first match
              • Shift+Enter Search for what’s typed instead
              • Esc Clear the box (again closes the popup); cancel a rename
              • ↓ ↑ From the box into the list and through its rows; ↑ on the first row goes back to the box
              • → ← On a row: open or close its card. On a tab: the next or previous tab
              • Any letter On a row: typing goes to the box
              • Right-click Shift+F10 On a quick link: rename, move or remove it

              Backup

              Everything Code Scout keeps, in one file: settings, quick links and sites, favourite videos, actresses and tags, seen codes, visit counts and the subtitle file picked for each video. For a reinstall, or to move to another computer what is kept on this one only.

              Importing replaces the settings, quick links and sites here with the file’s. Favourites, seen codes and subtitle choices from the file are added to what’s here; none are removed.
